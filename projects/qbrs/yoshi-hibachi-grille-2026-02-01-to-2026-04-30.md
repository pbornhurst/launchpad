## Key Findings

1. **Both locations grew 13%+ in sales, with Southfield's Marketplace channel nearly doubling.** Total combined sales grew from $31,932 to $36,266 (+13.6%) and total orders grew +22.6%. The standout is Southfield's Marketplace, which went from $2,985 to $5,900 (+99.9%), driven by aggressive promotional activity (Buy 1 Get 1, Spend $50 Get 20% Off) plus sponsored listings.

2. **Average order value softened across all channels.** Combined AOV dropped from $20.18 to $18.69 (-7.4%). The Marketplace AOV is still healthy at $26.20, but it slipped from $28.66. The in-store AOV decline to $17.11 is worth a closer look — it suggests either menu mix shift toward smaller items or a pricing review opportunity, particularly at Gratiot where in-store AOV fell to $17.26.

3. **Sponsored Listings are your highest-efficiency marketing channel — both stores delivered 7.7x+ ROAS.** Combined, sponsored placements drove $34,237 in sales on $4,245 in ad fees (ROAS 8.06), and brought in 232 new customers at a $18.30 cost per acquisition. Gratiot's ROAS of 8.50 is exceptional. April's expansion at Gratiot (24,998 impressions, up from 10,829) drove the location's strongest sales month.

4. **Southfield's "Buy 1, Get 1 Free" promotion is your largest single revenue driver but operates at marginal ROAS (2.37).** This promo drove 682 orders / $24,742 in sales but cost you $9,780 in discounts plus $675 in fees — a 42% discount rate against the promo's own sales. It acquired 221 new customers but with an effective cost of $47 per new customer through this promo alone. Worth testing a less aggressive structure (e.g., spend threshold instead of true BOGO).

5. **Gratiot is leaving Marketplace growth on the table.** Despite outperforming Southfield in absolute sales, Gratiot's Marketplace channel is flat year-over-period ($2,588 vs $2,632) and runs zero merchant-funded promotions. Its 17.71% menu conversion rate also trails Southfield's 24.30% by nearly 7 percentage points — a clear signal there's headroom to capture.

6. **Menu structure is inconsistent across locations.** Functionally identical proteins (chicken, steak, shrimp) are sold under different category names, different item names, and different price points across the two stores. Southfield uses "Hibachi Your Way" + mixed-case names; Gratiot uses "Mini/Full/Double/Triple Singles" + ALL CAPS. This makes brand consistency harder for repeat customers and complicates marketing creative.

7. **Menu conversion is improving and well above industry baseline.** Both locations finished April above 25% conversion (Southfield) and 19% (Gratiot), versus 18.25% blended in the prior period. Photo coverage at 85-87% is solid. The Apr 27 week dip is expected (partial week).

## Recommendations

### Pricing & Menu Engineering

- **Restructure the Southfield "Buy 1, Get 1 Free" promotion.** Replace with a "Spend $35, Get $7 Off" minimum-threshold offer. This should preserve order volume while improving the discount-to-sales ratio. Target ROAS: 4.0+ (vs current 2.37). Expected savings: roughly $3,000-4,000 per quarter on a similar volume of promotional orders.
- **Align menu structure across both locations.** Unify category names ("Hibachi Bowls" / "Hibachi Boxes" / "Fried Rice") and item naming conventions. This makes cross-location marketing easier and improves the customer experience for diners who visit both stores.
- **Audit "Open Item" SKU usage at Southfield** (115 SKUs / $1,982 in revenue). Consolidate frequently-used custom items into proper menu entries; remove or hide unused ones to improve menu navigation.

### Marketing Mix

- **Increase Sponsored Listings investment at both locations.** With ROAS of 7.7x (Southfield) and 8.5x (Gratiot), every additional ad dollar is creating $7-8 of incremental sales. Consider a 30-50% budget lift, particularly at Gratiot in February/March where impressions dropped below 12,000.
- **Launch a Gratiot promotional program.** Gratiot has zero promotional activity this period. Start with one targeted offer ("First Order $5 Off" or weekday lunch promo) to test response. Target: drive Marketplace orders from 95 to 150+ next quarter while keeping discount rate under 12%.
- **Maintain "Storefront Discount for Larger Orders"** — it's your highest-ROAS promotion (7.78x) and the most efficient new-customer acquisition vehicle ($31.71 per new customer). Consider extending the same offer concept to Gratiot.

### Operations & Customer Experience

- **Encourage post-delivery ratings.** Zero ratings captured for either location in 90 days is the most fixable item on this report. Implement a simple in-bag "Tap to Rate" card or a follow-up text where customer info is captured. Target: 30+ ratings per location over the next 90 days.
- **Investigate the in-store AOV decline at Gratiot** (down 9-12% across the comparison period). Check for menu changes, promotional spillover into in-store, or upsell opportunities at the counter (combo prompts, drink/side attach).

### Menu Conversion

- **Close the conversion gap at Gratiot.** Southfield converts at 24.30%; Gratiot converts at 17.71%. Targeted actions: review item photos on lowest-converting items, ensure top-revenue items are above the fold, simplify the "Mini Singles / Full Singles / Doubles / Triples" structure which may overwhelm new visitors.

---

## Growth Opportunities

- **Catering / Larger Order Capture:** The "Storefront Discount for Larger Orders" promo signals genuine demand for catering-style orders ($54+ AOV, 7.78 ROAS). Consider a dedicated catering menu or family-meal bundles with the same discount mechanism.
- **Customer Retention:** Repeat customers average just 1.11 orders per customer across both stores. A simple "Welcome Back" $5-off offer for customers who haven't ordered in 30 days could move this to 1.4x+.
- **Loyalty Program ROI:** Southfield's loyalty promo drove 69 orders at 2.92 ROAS but acquired 0 new customers (it's a retention tool). Continue, but evaluate the discount level against retention lift.
- **Gratiot Marketplace Expansion:** The single biggest unrealized opportunity. With identical Marketplace volume to a year ago but 84% in-store growth, the channel is materially under-monetized. A combined Sponsored Listing budget increase + first promotional campaign could realistically double Marketplace sales.
